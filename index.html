About

Management Software Market Sizing for PE Roll-Up Strategy

Commercial Due Diligence
Mid-market private equity firm • B2B Software (Services Industry)

Challenge

A PE firm evaluating a compliance software provider in a crowded mid-market niche needed to know: Was the addressable market large enough to support a roll-up strategy?

Approach

Built a bottom-up TAM/SAM/SOM model using ONS business population data, segmented by company size and sector. Developed pricing assumptions through 12 mystery shopping calls with competitors. Validated 3-5 year market forecasts through 5 expert interviews. Modelled competitive positioning and assessed AI disruption risk.

Outcome

Our forecasting showed stable demand with defensible positioning against AI threats. The PE firm proceeded with the acquisition, confident in the market's roll-up potential.

Venezuela Political Risk Assessment Through Human Intelligence

Expansion & Growth Strategy
Multinational corporation • Political Risk & Geopolitical Intelligence

Challenge

A corporate client considering Venezuelan market entry needed to assess political stability and operational risk in a low-data environment. Key questions: What was the real status of oil contract renegotiations? What was the likelihood of regime change? Which companies were successfully operating under current conditions?

Approach

Conducted 20 expert interviews with on-ground sources across Venezuela, including industry participants, political analysts, and local operators. Built a network-based intelligence picture where public data was unavailable. Assessed government stability indicators and mapped the current operational landscape for foreign companies.

Outcome

Delivered a comprehensive risk assessment identifying key operational challenges, regime stability forecasts, and a profile of companies successfully navigating the Venezuelan market. The analysis enabled the client to make an informed go/no-go decision with ground-truth intelligence unavailable through traditional research.

Data-Driven School Engagement Strategy for Education Services Provider

Strategic Impact & Insights
PE-backed education services company • Education & Food Services

Challenge

A leading school meal provider needed to demonstrate their value proposition to schools and systematize their engagement approach. They believed their service improved student outcomes but lacked quantitative evidence and a scalable strategy framework.

Approach

Built Python-based benchmarking model comparing client schools against national GCSE and attendance averages. Ran regression analysis demonstrating statistically significant performance improvements. Surveyed 500 students (UK-representative sample, 100 target population) to identify drivers of success. Applied cluster analysis to identify 8 distinct school archetypes and 6 student personas, creating a matrix of ~100 tailored engagement strategies.

Outcome

Delivered evidence-based playbook to CEO, CFO, COO, and Director of Food demonstrating measurable impact and providing actionable segmentation strategy for both new and existing client schools.

UK 3PL Market Intelligence & Competitive Positioning

Strategic Impact & Insights
Global logistics leader • Third-Party Logistics (3PL)

Challenge

A global logistics leader needed to understand market share dynamics in the UK 3PL sector to inform strategic planning. Key question: How was the competitive landscape evolving, and where did the client stand relative to emerging players?

Approach

Built proprietary market sizing model by identifying all UK 3PL providers with £10M+ revenue through online research and Companies House filings. Extracted financial data to create comprehensive market calculator. Analyzed 5-year historical trends and modeled 3-year forward trajectory to assess market share evolution by company size segment.

Outcome

Analysis revealed the client had lost share to competitors in key segments. Findings informed regional board decision to revise organic growth targets and adjust competitive strategy accordingly.

International Expansion: Choosing Markets and Routes to Market

Expansion & Growth Strategy
UK manufacturing firm • B2B Industrial Products
30expert interviews (20 US, 10 Japan)
3,000+open-source documents analysed
25step commercial supply chain mapped
5person US commercial team now in place

Challenge

A UK-based manufacturer was seeking a multi-country international expansion in a highly competitive market, where its competitors were pursuing similarly aggressive international growth. The firm still had limited knowledge of international markets and wanted help segmenting the ideal countries to penetrate, understanding the current competitive landscape, identifying the optimal sales approach for these markets, and assessing the regulatory implications for product design.

Approach

Conducted 30 expert interviews (20 in the US and 10 in Japan) with buyers, distributors and industry specialists, and analysed over 3,000 open-source documents covering legislation, standards and competitors. Mapped the 25-step commercial supply chain in the target market, so the client understood exactly how a sale happens, from first contact to order. Reviewed local regulation to see which products could be sold as they are and which would need changes, and compared direct and distributor-led models market by market.

Outcome

We narrowed down the areas for geographic growth and advised on which countries to disregard. We recommended entering the US, a large and established economy that was still growing, where buildings are rebuilt and renewed more often, creating a steady flow of new opportunities. We advised against Japan, a more mature and saturated market where long sales cycles would have tied up investment for little return.

The client acted on the advice. They did not pursue Japan, and they have since built a five-person commercial team in the US.

Scaling a Partner Programme Across 20+ Markets

Expansion & Growth Strategy
Global technology company • Partnerships & Go-to-Market
$200M+additional revenue generated globally
20+markets entered across three regions
8,000partnership opportunities mapped in the Americas
5 yrsof partnership with the client

Challenge

A global technology company already served its largest customers directly, and had almost the whole top end of the market covered. The only room left for growth was the long tail of small businesses, which a direct sales team could never reach. The company had developed a smart answer: partner with the key platforms those small businesses already use, and reach them through those partners. The method was designed but had never been scaled. The client needed to take it global, market by market.

Approach

Piloted the methodology in the Americas (US, Canada, Brazil, Argentina and Colombia), building a complete long list of about 8,000 partnership opportunities. We then recategorised the list and structured the categories for outreach, turning a routine list-building exercise into a forward-looking database of partners. With the US as a benchmark for a mature market and Brazil for a fast-growing one, we could show the client what to expect in any new market before entering it. We then applied the same data-led playbook across Asia-Pacific (Australia, New Zealand, India, Singapore, Vietnam, Thailand and South Korea) and Europe (France, Spain, Germany, Switzerland, Austria and the Nordics).

Outcome

The programme reached more than 20 markets and generated over $200 million in additional revenue for the client globally. It also left the client with a baseline for future campaigns and a new methodology for deciding how to approach new markets. The relationship lasted around five years.

Validating a Buy-and-Build Thesis for a PE-Backed Law Firm

Commercial Due Diligence
PE-backed legal services firm • Acquisition Strategy
120legal providers mapped and scored
10expert interviews
3business models compared
2clear routes to growth identified

Challenge

A law firm had just been bought by a new private equity owner. The investment thesis relied on adding specific legal services to the firm's existing offer, mainly through acquisition. Before committing, the owner needed to know whether the thesis had legs: which providers existed, which were worth buying, and what a successful target looked like.

Approach

Started with the business model, breaking the market into three types of provider: pure technology, pure lawyer-led and hybrid. Built a long list of 120 legal providers in the target area and a scoring matrix to evaluate each one's potential, finding the sweet spot for each model. Ran 10 expert interviews to understand margin structures, client acquisition pipelines and market trends. We presented the findings to the management team, who carried them to the investment committee and the board.

Outcome

We showed that no provider matched everything the client wanted. It was a clear value versus volume trade-off: pure lawyer-led firms had the highest margins, while the technology platforms had the largest client bases. We identified the companies that did each best. That gave the owner a clear choice between two acquisition routes, instead of a search for a target that did not exist. The client went on to approach most of the targets on our list.
